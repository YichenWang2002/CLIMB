CLIMB

Curriculum Learning for Multi-agent Behavior Tree Generation with Small Language Models

Yichen Wang1, Zhongxuan Cai1,*, Yinuo Liu1, Yuhao Wang1, Tianjian Jiang1, Yuanxi Peng1
1College of Computer Science and Technology, National University of Defense Technology, Changsha, China
Paper Code Dataset

Abstract

Behavior trees have become a popular approach for modeling robot behaviors through recursive tick signals, but their automated construction remains challenging. Recent work explores using large language models (LLMs) to generate behavior trees from natural-language task descriptions. However, in multi-agent coordination scenarios, LLMs face critical challenges: they struggle to learn coordination primitives, are too large for practical deployment, and lack spatial cognition, causing task failures. Addressing these challenges, we present CLIMB (Curriculum Learning for Multi-agent Behavior Tree generation with small language models): at the supervision stage, we leverage symbolic planning algorithms to construct high-quality training data encoding correct multi-agent cooperation priors; we further optimize both training and inference stages — at the training stage, we design a curriculum learning strategy that identifies coordination complexity from both difficulty and utility perspectives to progressively improve learning effectiveness; at the inference stage, we provide the model with spatial cognition to prevent generating spatially unreachable behaviors. We validate our approach through both numerical simulation and physical 3D simulation in ROS 2/Gazebo, demonstrating that our method significantly improves execution success rate compared to standard supervised fine-tuning, and our fine-tuned 1B small language model outperforms current mainstream LLMs while reducing token cost.

Method Overview

  • Planner-grounded supervision. Training targets are behavior trees compiled from verified symbolic plans and validated by the shared executor — every supervised example is executable by construction.
  • SPCL curriculum. Training tasks are staged by difficulty and utility of their coordination structure, from single-robot sequences to tightly coupled three-agent missions.
  • SCD decoding. Enhances the model's spatial awareness at inference: decoding is grounded in the platform map, so tokens referencing locations or edges absent from it are masked and spatially unreachable behaviors can no longer be generated.

Dataset

Every pair is generated by a planning pipeline rather than mined or hand-written. A scenario sampler first draws a task — facts, goals, connectivity and faults — from a STRIPS domain; a grounded forward planner computes a valid action plan; a deterministic compiler turns the plan into an executable BehaviorTree.CPP v4 XML; and an executor replay gate re-runs the tree under the injected faults — candidates that fail are rejected and the task resampled. Only verified plans survive, and an LLM paraphrases their symbol facts into a fluent natural-language mission, yielding pairs whose targets are correct by construction.

The released dataset contains 6,000 training and 600 validation missions from four seen domains (warehouse, hospital, search&rescue, office) and a 480-task held-out test set from two unseen domains (library, greenhouse): 320 relay-transport and 160 joint-heavy-transport missions, 357 of them with recoverable faults. Each instance pairs a natural-language mission with a gold executable XML, and success requires the shared symbolic executor to tick the tree to SUCCESS while every goal fact holds.

Sample record

One training instance. The instruction is a fixed system prompt shared by the whole dataset; the input is the natural-language mission given to the model; the output is the gold, executor-validated behavior tree the model must emit.

Instruction (shared system prompt)

You are a helpful assistant that creates behavior trees for multi-robot teams. Your task: - Convert the provided natural-language mission description into an XML-formatted multi-agent behavior tree. - The behavior tree must be compatible with the BehaviorTree.CPP library (<root BTCPP_format="4">), with one <BehaviorTree> per robot coordinated by a main tree. - Use Fallback nodes to recover from the failures mentioned in the mission. - Use SignalReady/WaitReady and the Handover/Co* joint nodes for inter-robot coordination. Output Requirements: - Output only the XML representation of the behavior tree. Do not include explanations, comments, or any additional text. - Every robot mentioned in the mission must have its own <BehaviorTree ID="Agent_<name>">.

Input (natural-language mission)

Alpha (id alpha) and Beta (id beta) are a two-robot team stationed at shelf B (shelf_b). Their task: retrieve the blue crate (crate_blue) from shelf C (shelf_c) and deliver it to the charging bay (charge_bay). Alpha is cleared to work only on shelf C and shelf B; Beta is cleared to work only on shelf B and the charging bay. The handover of the blue crate between them may fumble on the first attempt, so they should be ready to try again. The warehouse layout connects the charging bay to shelf B, shelf B to shelf A (shelf_a) and shelf C, shelf A to the loading dock (loading_dock) and the packing station (packing_station), and the loading dock and packing station to each other. The charging bay is the only charging station.

Demo: mission → executable behavior tree

1. Three-agent relay with a transient handover fault

Sunlight fell across the public library as three robots prepared in whispered motors. Alpha (id alpha) stood at the front desk (front_desk), where the rare manuscript (rare_manuscript) rested under a glass dome. Beta (id beta) hummed at the charging pod (charge_pod), the library’s only charging station, and Gamma (id gamma) waited in the archive room (archive_room). Their mission: carry the manuscript to the archive room, no matter what. Each robot had strict territory. Alpha could only work between the front desk and the reading hall (reading_hall). Beta moved between the reading hall and the charging pod… Execute.

The gold tree encodes the rendezvous protocol (SignalReady/WaitReady + HandoverGive/HandoverTake) and a retry fallback at the fault-prone handover.

2. Two-robot cooperative transport

Hey robot crew, quick one from facilities. Alpha (alpha), you’re starting at the charging pod (charge_pod). Beta (beta), you’re in the archive room (archive_room). We need the heavy oak bookcase (oak_bookcase_heavy) moved from the front desk (front_desk) to the charging pod. Watch out: the corridor between the archive room and the front desk could have debris. If a robot is at the archive room, clear it with ClearPath before trying to pass. For routing: the archive room connects to the charging pod and the front desk. The charging pod connects back to the archive room and to the restoration lab (restoration_lab)… Execute.

Joint CoPickUp/CoMoveTo/CoPlaceDown leaves appear in both agents' subtrees and synchronize at the executor.

3. Relay with a blocked corridor (fault-aware recovery)

Alpha, Beta, Gamma: initiate joint retrieval and cataloging operation. The encyclopedia set (encyclopedia_set) is waiting in the book return bin (return_bin). Alpha (alpha), you start there. Beta (beta), you start at the front desk (front_desk). Gamma (gamma), you start in the archive room (archive_room). Your mission: transport the encyclopedia_set to the archive_room and catalog it there with CatalogBook. Recall operating boundaries. Alpha: return_bin and front_desk only. Beta: front_desk and charging pod (charge_pod) only. Gamma: charge_pod and archive_room only. Beware the passage between return_bin and front_desk may be blocked by debris… Execute.

Fallback branches wrap risky transitions: ClearPath then retry when the mission warns of debris.
